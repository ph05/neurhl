A simulator that lost the game and won the season

Built a puck-level event simulator to predict NHL games. It failed at the thing it was built for, beat the incumbent at something else, and the most useful output was five bugs that every validation gate passed straight through.

Headline: the engine does not improve game-by-game prediction

Per-game log loss was the directed primary metric. A plain Elo rating beats the simulator, and beats the simulator blended with Elo. The preregistered G-STOP rule fired, so the held-out confirmation window was never spent.

  • Constant (home rate)0.68942
  • Simulator alone0.68416

  • Elo alone0.67668
  • v1 hierarchical (incumbent)0.67314

Log loss, lower is better. Pooled over 4,920 held-out games (2014–2017). Bar length shows distance above the incumbent.

The blend is constructed to nest Elo — set one coefficient to zero and it reduces to recalibrated Elo — so it cannot do worse than the incumbent by construction. It doesn't do better either. Expected goals were well calibrated throughout (2.87–2.90 against an actual 2.82–2.92, with correct home advantage), so this isn't a broken rate model. Per-game outcome variance simply swamps the refinement the engine supplies.

03Five things the gates could not see

This is the part worth keeping. Two data leaks and three modelling bugs all survived the preregistered realism gates. Every one was caught downstream — by a consumer that had to supply its own inputs, or by a reader who looked at an output and said it could not be right.

The model was told who touches the puck next

leak

The on-ice encoder oriented its “for/against” pooling by a flag equal to the owning team of the next event — the team half of its own prediction target.

agreement with target team 1.0000 (zero off-diagonal)

A personnel change announces the faceoff that caused it

leak

Conditioning on the next on-ice set leaked harder. Line changes happen at stoppages, so a change in on-ice composition is the announcement of the faceoff that produced it.

Δ −1 skater → P(faceoff next) 0.986 base 0.199 lift 4.96 Δ −2 skaters → 0.992 lift 4.99 Δ +1 skater → P(penalty next) 0.077 base 0.028 lift 2.76

Team strength averaged over nine years

bug

Team rates pooled the entire training window, so by 2017 a club's attack rate was a nine-season average of a roster that had turned over repeatedly. The signature was accuracy decaying as more data arrived.

standings MAE 7.09 / 7.19 at vantages 2011 / 2012 (3 seasons pooled) 11.14–12.71 at 2014–2017 (9 seasons pooled)

Certainty that was never earned

bug

Only game-outcome noise was simulated, never uncertainty in team strength itself, so season intervals were far too narrow. The tell that the missing variance was real rather than a fudge: adding it improved every metric at once.

σ coverage MAE CRPS 0.00 0.639 10.01 7.53 0.07 0.794 9.59 6.94 ← adopted 0.13 0.917 9.56 7.08

Every player projected into a colder era

bug

The first player projection put the 2026-27 league leader at 97 points. Five compounding errors, each a constant chosen by hand instead of estimated — and the backtest could not see any of them, because it only ever tested 2014–2017 vantages, all inside the low-scoring era.

era raw per-60 rates averaged across a 13% league shift shrinkage one constant pulled a 3,300-min veteran 31% to the mean ice time modelled per player, ignoring a fixed 300 min/team-game budget position forwards (181 min) and defence (117) share one pool -> 28.4 min/gm schedule 48- and 56-game seasons read as "this player plays 56 games" projected leader 97.4 -> 123.2 backtest MAE 9.55 vs 18.54 baseline

The lesson generalises past this project. A realism gate scores a model using the same inputs the model trained on, so a leaked input is structurally invisible to it. Both event-realism gates passed cleanly with both leaks present and would have passed indefinitely. Detecting leakage needs either a consumer that cannot cheat, or a direct causality test: corrupt everything after a cut point, rebuild the inputs, and require them to be bit-identical.

06What was built, and what wasn't

Data recovered

Exact on-ice data now covers every season 2008–2026 — 18.9M shifts over 23,883 games. The pre-2012 coordinate hole is closed: the NHL feed carries no shot location before 2012, and 2.08M parsed shots fill it, verified to within one foot on seasons where both sources exist.

A parser bug was invented shifts for every goalie and fourth-liner; fixing it moved agreement with the independent feed from 0.981 to 0.99969, mean error 51.1s → 0.32s.

Not built

The deployment (line-change) model, the goalie starter model, and the graph layer over teammate pairs were all left unbuilt. The confirmatory test was blocked by G-STOP — correctly, and the window remains unspent for a future attempt.

Known limitations

xG calibration fails in the top decile. RAPM was never shown better than a one-line within-team adjustment. Assists are missing for 2008–2011, cause identified but unfixed. Player projections do not use the network's actor head, for want of a deployment model.

33 of 33 acceptance checks pass, each re-deriving its verdict from recorded numbers rather than trusting a report. Report-only: production scoring is unchanged.
