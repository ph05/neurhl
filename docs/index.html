Methodology

Data
NHL play-by-play and shift charts, 2008-09 to 2025-26; shot locations and expected goals from a gradient-boosted shot model recalibrated in season; daily roster snapshots from the NHL; lineups from NHL game reports and Daily Faceoff; injuries from MoneyPuck's injury list, with undated entries researched individually; players' records in other leagues (AHL, CHL, NCAA, USHL, KHL, SHL, Liiga and others) from the NHL's player records. No betting-market data enters any model.
Game engine
For every game the engine takes the two dressed lineups and each skater's and goalie's state (exponentially weighted rates, career totals, adjusted plus-minus priors). Neural heads adjust shrunk-history baselines for each skater; a deterministic team layer allocates ice time by strength, builds team scoring rates from the players on the ice, and splits team totals back to players so that player and team numbers add up exactly. Win probabilities come from integrating a score-and-time-dependent goal hazard over the game. Rookies and players with little NHL history also receive their records from other leagues, translated to NHL terms with league factors estimated from past moves.
Final probability
A logistic regression combining the game model, a neural player-level model and Elo, fitted on out-of-sample predictions from earlier seasons.
Lineups and availability
For the season projection, each team's roster is sampled game by game: a model of each skater's probability of dressing, injured players out until their return date, call-ups filling gaps, and goalie start shares with a back-to-back adjustment. Game-day forecasts use the posted or projected lineup instead.
Season-level adjustments
The season projection assembles every game from each player's and team's opening-night inputs, which carry the opening weeks' higher rate of power plays into all 84 games. On eleven past seasons, power-play opportunities from these inputs ran 33% above the season totals and power-play time 24% above. Power-play opportunities, power-play time and expected goals are therefore scaled by ratios estimated on 2011-12 to 2017-18 (0.776, 0.826, 0.961) and checked on 2018-19 to 2023-24. The model predicts regulation goals; season totals add expected overtime goals and shootout-deciding goals from each game's probability of going past regulation. The goal level is set so that projected regulation goals over the whole schedule match 2025-26.
Season simulation
Each simulated season draws one strength shock per team (standard deviation 0.07 on the log goal rate), plays every game from its probabilities, and applies the NHL's standings, tiebreakers and playoff format. A walk-forward backtest on 2019-2024 found this setting calibrated: 80% intervals covered 82% of team point totals.
Stat-sheet distributions
Box scores are simulated from the model means. The dispersion of team shots, expected goals and power-play opportunities, and the slope of goal and expected-goal means, were fitted by maximum likelihood on out-of-sample seasons and are scored against the unfitted versions at the end of the season.
Evaluation
Model versions and evaluation rules are fixed before the games they are evaluated on, and season prediction files are identified by SHA-256 hashes. Game forecasts are committed to the repository before puck drop. Season accuracy is evaluated once, after the last regular-season game; figures during the season are provisional.
